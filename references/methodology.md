# Workshop methodology

This is a concise, editable representation of the Clickmedia workshop slides dated 22 September 2026. It is not a verbatim copy of the presentation. Add speaker context from the workshop audio under a dated section rather than rewriting slide-derived guidance silently.

## Five-stage route

| Stage | Question answered | Working result |
|---|---|---|
| Segmentation and prioritization | Who needs this? | Ranked JTBD segments and a primary segment |
| Offer | What exactly do we propose? | Obstacle-to-mechanism offer options |
| Landing-page structure | Why would a person act? | Persuasion sequence and page blocks |
| Landing-page creation | Can the idea be expressed as a usable page? | Final copy and, if requested, responsive HTML |
| Analysis and iteration | What must improve before traffic? | Audit, fixes, and test-readiness verdict |

## Prompt patterns

### 1. Segment and prioritize

Act as a JTBD strategist. Given the product and market, generate 7-10 hypothetical segments. For each, state the situation, desired progress, and why this product may be hired. Score market size, value, profitability, and scalability. Recommend the top five and explain the ranking. If no region is supplied, use the CIS market as an assumption.

### 2. Create an offer

Act as a Value Proposition Canvas strategist. For the selected segment and product, identify what prevents the customer from reaching the desired outcome. Map a concrete product mechanism to that obstacle. Draft three offers. If the product does not address the obstacle, say so plainly.

### 3. Fill the landing-page structure

Act as a direct copywriter and marketer. Using the chosen segment and offer, write page copy. Each block must support motivation, ability to act, or a trigger. Use a direct tone and avoid clichés.

### 4. Create a landing page

Act as a frontend developer and UX/UI designer. Turn the approved copy into a working HTML page. Make it responsive, use clear placeholder media where assets are unavailable, and do not replace final copy with filler text.

### 5. Audit the landing page

Act as a conversion-page auditor. Review category and market cues, first screen, page logic, design, mobile experience, and trust/CTA. Label each claim as evidence, objective observation, or hypothesis.

### 6. Improve from the audit

Use the existing workshop context: segment, offer, copy, page, and audit. Make targeted revisions based on the audit rather than starting from zero.

## Landing-page blocks

Adapt the blocks to the product. Omit a block only when it cannot serve the chosen hypothesis.

1. One-line essence: why this is relevant now.
2. Path to the desired outcome: understandable steps.
3. Early value: an immediate felt benefit or useful first result.
4. Value by customer job: how the product supports each important job.
5. Recognition: how the visitor sees their situation in the page.
6. Delivery: why the product can credibly do what it promises.
7. Outcome image: what changes after success.
8. Barriers: specific objections and grounded responses.
9. Alternatives: why the chosen route differs from credible alternatives.

## Audit rubric

| Dimension | Review question |
|---|---|
| Market | Does the page reflect how the category and audience actually make decisions? |
| First screen | Is the offer understandable at first glance? |
| Page logic | Does the page answer customer questions in a useful order? |
| Design | Does form help the message instead of distracting from it? |
| Mobile | Does the mobile page work as its own experience? |
| Trust and CTA | Are reasons to believe and the next action clear? |
